Species Description

The Hercules beetle (Dynastes hercules) is the most famous and largest of the rhinoceros beetles. Native to the rainforests of Central America, South America, and the Lesser Antilles, it is remarkably large, with some males reaching up to 17.5 cm (about 7 inches) in length including their horns.

Their most striking feature is the pair of large horns found on the males. One horn projects forward from the thorax, and a second horn projects forward from the head. These horns can grow longer than the beetle's actual body. They are used in combat with other males to establish dominance and secure mating rights with females.

Habitat & Ecology

Primary inhabitants of tropical rainforests. They are nocturnal and are often attracted to light sources. Larvae develop within decaying tree trunks for up to 2 years.
